## Part A — Actual Previous-Year Questions (Verbatim from 2020–2023)

> Source: MATH-1204 Year-Final papers 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 (image extraction). Marks as in original. LaTeX added for readability.

### 2020 — MATH-1204

**Q3(b) — [4 marks]**
Find the length of the Arc of the curve $$p^2 = ar$$ ; from $$r = a$$ to $$r = 2a$$

**Q3(c) — [6 marks]**
Find the surface area of the solid generated by revolving the cycloid $$x = a(\theta + \sin\theta),\; y = a(1 + \cos\theta)$$ about its base.

**Q4(c) — [4 marks]**
Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{(x-\alpha)(\beta - x)}\,dx$$

**Q5 — [3.5×4=14 marks] — Evaluate (any four):**
(i) $$\displaystyle \int e^{2x}\left(\frac{1+\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x}\right)dx$$
(ii) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{2x^2-1}{(x+1)^2(x-2)}dx$$
(iii) $$\displaystyle \int x\sqrt{\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}}dx$$
(iv) $$\displaystyle \int (\sin^{-1} x)^2 dx$$

**Q7(c) — [5 marks]**
Show that the arc length of the parabola $$r(1+\cos\theta)=2$$ from $$\theta=0$$ to $$\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$$ is $$\sqrt{2}+\ln(1+\sqrt{2})$$

### 2021 — MATH-1204

**Q1(a) — [2×2=4 marks] — Integrate:**
(i) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{x+\sin x}{1+\cos x}dx$$
(ii) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{dx}{\sin^{1/2}x \cos^{7/2}x}$$

**Q1(b) — [4 marks]**
Find the surface area of the solid generated by revolving the parabola $$y^2 = 4ax$$ between $$x=0$$ and $$x=a$$ about x-axis.

**Q1(c) — [3×2=6 marks] — Evaluate (for any two):**
(i) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}}{x^3}dx$$
(ii) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{dx}{5+4\sin 2x}$$
(iii) $$\displaystyle \int \tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}\,dx$$

**Q5(a) — [5 marks]**
Show that the arc length of the Curve $$y = \ln \sec x$$ from $$x=0$$ to $$x=\frac{\pi}{3}$$ is $$\ln(2+\sqrt{3})$$

**Q6(c) — [5 marks]**
Find the perimeter of the cardioid $$r = 2(1-\cos\theta)$$

**Q7(c) — [5 marks] — (also listed under DE)**
Evaluate the improper integral $$\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^4+1}dx$$

### 2022 — MATH-1204

**Q1(a) — [8 marks] — A curve C is defined by parametric equation $$x=t^2, y=t^3-3t$$**
(i) Show that C has two tangents at the point (3,0) and find their equations.
(ii) Find the points on C where the tangent is horizontal or vertical.
(iii) Sketch the curve.
*(Included here as arc-length / parametric context; also relevant to differential calculus)*

**Q3(b) — [5 marks]**
If the ellipse $$x^2+4y^2=16$$ is rotated about its minor axis, the resulting ellipsoid is called an oblate spheroid. Find the surface area of this ellipsoid.

**Q6(a) — [3×3=9 marks] — Evaluate:**
I) $$\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \cos^3 x (\sin x)^{1/2} dx$$
II) $$\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^2+1}dx$$ *(as printed; standard form is x/(x^4+1) — see 2021/2023 variants; attempt as written and note convergence)*
III) $$\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\infty} x e^{-x^2} dx$$

**Q7(a) — [5 marks]**
Find the area of the region that lies inside the circle $$r=3\sin\theta$$ and outside the cardioid $$r=1+\sin\theta$$

**Q7(b) — [5 marks]**
Find the length of one arch of the cycloid $$x=r(\theta-\sin\theta),\; y=r(1-\cos\theta)$$

### 2023 — MATH-1204

**Q1(a) — [6 marks] — Evaluate:**
(i) $$\displaystyle \int (\sin^{-1} x)^2 dx$$
(ii) $$\displaystyle \int \sin^4 x \cos^3 x \,dx$$

**Q1(c) — [3 marks]**
Evaluate the improper integral $$\displaystyle \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}dx$$

> **Note on duplication:** $$\int (\sin^{-1}x)^2 dx$$ appears in both 2020-Q5(iv) and 2023-Q1(a)(i). $$\int \frac{x}{x^4+1}$$ improper integral recurs in 2021-Q7(c), 2022-Q6(a)(II), with variant limits.

## Part B — 10 Original Practice Questions (Exam-Realistic)

> Same style and marks as MATH-1204 finals. Attempt without answers first. Answers in companion file.

### [Easy]

**P1. [4 marks] [Easy]**
Evaluate $$\displaystyle I_1=\int \frac{x+\sin x}{1+\cos x}dx$$ and $$\displaystyle I_2=\int \frac{dx}{\sin^{1/2}x\cos^{7/2}x}$$ . Hint for I1: write $$x\frac{1}{1+\cos x}+ \frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}$$ and use $$1+\cos x =2\cos^2(x/2)$$.

**P2. [3 marks] [Easy]**
Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}$$ and $$\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\infty} x e^{-x^2}dx$$ . Determine convergence/divergence with full improper-limit reasoning.

**P3. [4 marks] [Easy]**
Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int \sin^4 x \cos^3 x\,dx$$ by substituting $$u=\sin x$$. Hence deduce $$\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^4 x\cos^3 x\,dx$$.

### [Medium]

**P4. [5 marks] [Medium]**
Find the surface area generated by revolving the parabola $$y^2=4ax$$ from $$x=0$$ to $$x=a$$ about the x-axis. Show that $$S = \frac{8\pi a^2}{3}(2\sqrt2-1)$$ .

**P5. [6 marks] [Medium]**
Evaluate:
(i) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}}{x^3}dx\;(x>a>0)$$  (ii) $$\displaystyle \int \frac{dx}{5+4\sin 2x}$$ using $$t=\tan x$$ or $$u=\tan x$$ .

**P6. [5 marks] [Medium]**
Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^3 x (\sin x)^{1/2} dx$$ using Beta function or elementary substitution $$u=\sin x$$.

### [Hard]

**P7. [6 marks] [Hard]**
Show that length of one arch of the cycloid $$x=r(\theta-\sin\theta),\,y=r(1-\cos\theta),\ 0\le\theta\le2\pi$$ is $$8r$$. Also find the surface area generated by revolving the cycloid $$x=a(\theta+\sin\theta), y=a(1+\cos\theta)$$ about its base (x-axis), and verify it equals $$\frac{64\pi a^2}{3}$$.

**P8. [7 marks] [Hard]**
(a) Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sqrt{(x-\alpha)(\beta-x)}dx = \frac{\pi}{8}(\beta-\alpha)^2$$ .
(b) The ellipse $$\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1$$ (i.e. $$x^2+4y^2=16$$) is rotated about its minor axis (y-axis). Derive and evaluate the surface area of the oblate spheroid: $$S = 2\pi a^2\left(1+\frac{1-e^2}{e}\tanh^{-1}e\right)$$ with $$a=4, b=2$$ and compute numeric value.

**P9. [6 marks] [Hard]**
(a) Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int x\sqrt{\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}}dx$$ by substituting $$x^2=\cos 2t$$ or $$u=x^2$$.
(b) Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int e^{2x}\frac{1+\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x}dx$$ .
(c) Evaluate $$\displaystyle \int \tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}\,dx$$ by parts.

**P10. [6 marks] [Hard]**
Find the area inside $$r=3\sin\theta$$ and outside $$r=1+\sin\theta$$. Sketch the curves, find intersection points $$\theta=\pi/6,5\pi/6$$, and evaluate $$A=\frac12\int (r_{\text{outer}}^2-r_{\text{inner}}^2)d\theta$$.
