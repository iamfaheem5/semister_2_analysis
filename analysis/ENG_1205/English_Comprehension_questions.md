## Part A — Actual Previous-Year Questions (Verbatim 2020–2023)

### 2020 — ENG-1205

**Passage — [10 marks comprehension]** *(Literature & Philosophy)*

> There was a time when the study of literature and philosophy formed the basis of education. Knowledge of science gradually became indispensable in man's life. The question then arose, should literature continue to occupy its privileged position in our scheme of education in college and universities? There is no doubt that in the modern world we cannot think of doing without science. No one, therefore, would object to giving to science a place of prime importance in our curricula of studies. But that should in no way affect the position of literature. It is something as important, and there is no inherent enmity between the two.
> 
> Literature deals imaginatively with the stuff of which life is made. The study of what the men of letters have thought and felt has permanent interest for men. Literature gives us the unique experience of being in contact with a distinct personality which expresses itself through some "familiar matter" of real life or some fantasy. Science, on the other hand, deals with impersonal generalisation of universal principles. Literature studies from a personal point of view. It humanises every fact of life.

**Q1 — [1×5=5 marks] Answer the following questions:**
(a) What does literature deal with?
(b) What, according to the passage, is the basis of education?
(c) What is the function of literature?
(d) What is the theme of the passage?
(e) What type of relation does the passage instigate in the passage?

**Q2 — [1×5=5 marks] Write the meaning of the following words in English.**
(a) indispensable; (b) privileged; (c) distinct; (d) enmity; (e) fantasy.

**Q3 — [5 marks]**
Write the précis of the given passage.

### 2021 — ENG-1205

**Passage — Good Governance (15 marks)**

> Good governance is the process of measuring how public institutions conduct public affairs and manage public resources and guarantee the realization of human rights in a manner essentially free of abuse and corruption and with due regard for the rule of law. Governance is "the process of decision-making and the process by which decisions are implemented (or not implemented)". Governance in this context can apply to corporate, international, national, or local governance as well as the interactions between other sectors of society.
> 
> The concept of "good governance" thus emerges as a model to compare ineffective economies or political bodies with viable economies and political bodies. The concept centres on the responsibility of governments and governing bodies to meet the needs of the masses as opposed to select groups in society. Because countries often described as "most successful" are liberal democratic states, concentrated in Europe and the Americas, good governance standards often measure other state institutions against these states. Aid organizations and the authorities of developed countries often will focus the meaning of "good governance" to a set of requirements that conform to the organization's agenda, making "good governance" imply many different things in many different contexts.

**Q1 — [5 marks] Answer the following questions:**
a) What is the central idea of the given passage?
b) What are the factors that good governance includes?
c) What is the responsibility of the government?
d) What do the aid organizations and developed countries do?
e) What does good governance mean?

**Q2 — [5 marks] Write the meaning of the following words in English.**
a) viable  b) measure  c) agenda  d) emerge  e) requirement.

**Q3 — [5 marks]**
Write the précis of the given passage.

### 2022 — ENG-1205

**Passage — Internationalization of Higher Education (15 marks)**

> The internationalization of higher education stands as an intricate phenomenon that reflects the profound transformation of academic landscapes in an increasingly interconnected world. At its core, this paradigm denotes the transcendence of traditional geographic and cultural boundaries in the pursuit of knowledge and intellectual enrichment. Universities and colleges across the globe are reorienting their strategies to not only welcome a diverse spectrum of students and scholars from around the world but also to establish international partnerships and collaborative research initiatives that bolster the cross-pollination of ideas. This seismic shift towards internationalization underscores the recognition that addressing the complex challenges of the modern era, such as climate change, global health crises, and socio-economic disparities, requires a synthesis of diverse perspectives and expertise. Moreover, the internationalization of higher education is fostering an enriching tapestry of multiculturalism, promoting cross-cultural understanding, and preparing students to navigate the complex globalized landscapes of the 21st century.
> However, as the trajectory of internationalization unfolds, it is not devoid of challenges. Educational institutions must contend with issues such as equitable access to global educational opportunities and the maintenance of academic quality in a diverse learning environment. The promotion of linguistic and cultural diversity is a key facet of internationalization, but it necessitates addressing language barriers and ensuring that students from various linguistic backgrounds can fully engage in academic discourse. Additionally, there is a growing need for institutional and governmental policies that facilitate the integration of international students, thus optimizing their educational experience. Furthermore, the internationalization of higher education is inextricably linked to concerns regarding the commodification of knowledge, as the pursuit of profit can sometimes undermine the broader educational mission. In navigating these complexities, higher education institutions must adopt a nuanced approach that embraces the manifold benefits of internationalization while vigilantly addressing its inherent challenges.

**Q1(a) — [5 marks] Choose the correct answer:**
i. What is the primary impact of educational internationalization?
a) Pedagogical tradition  b) Academic homogeneity  c) Paradigm alteration  d) Globalisolation
ii. When considering the challenges of internationalization, what issue significantly perturbs academic institutions?
a) Profit maximization  b) Linguistic diversity  c) Equitable access  d) Disciplinary synergy
iii. What is the relationship between the push for linguistic and cultural diversity and language barriers?
a) Synergy  b) Irrelevance  c) Antagonism  d) Concordance
iv. How can institutions enhance the integration of international students?
a) Pedagogical isolation  b) Cultivating xenophobia  c) Fostering intercultural interactions  d) Encouraging insularity
v. In the context of the commercialization of knowledge, what is the most judicious approach for addressing concerns?
a) Unbridled capitalism  b) Pragmatic acknowledgement  c) Profit maximization  d) Academic isolation

**Q1(b) — [5 marks] Answer the following questions:**
i. What's the core concept of internationalization in education, and how does it challenge traditional boundaries?
ii. What are the main challenges faced by institutions aiming for internationalization, particularly in terms of access and quality?

### 2023 — ENG-1205

**Passage — Philadelphia Murals (15 marks)**

> Philadelphia is a city known for many things. It is where the Declaration of Independence was signed in 1776, and it was also the first capital of the United States. But one fact about Philadelphia is not so well-known: it is home to nearly 3,000 murals painted on the sides of homes and buildings around the city. It is said that Philadelphia has more murals than any other city in the world, with the exception of Rome. How did this come to be?
> More than 20 years ago, a New Jersey artist named Jane Golden started a program pairing troubled youth with artists to paint murals on a few buildings around the city. From this small project, something magical happened. The young people involved helped to create magnificent pieces of art, but there were other, perhaps more important, benefits. The young people learned to collaborate and get along with many different kinds of people during the various steps required to paint and design a mural. They learned to be responsible, because they needed to follow a schedule to make sure the murals were completed. They also learned to take pride in their community. It is hard for any resident to see the spectacular designs and not feel proud to be a part of Philadelphia.
> Take a walk around some of the poorest neighborhoods in Philadelphia—neighborhoods full of broken windows and littered front steps—and you will find beautiful works of art on the sides and fronts of buildings. But murals are not only found in poor neighborhoods. They are found in affluent ones as well. Special buses take tourists to different parts of the city to see the various murals, which range from huge portraits of historical heroes, to cityscapes, to scenes depicting the diverse ethnic groups that call Philadelphia home.
> As a result of its success, the mural program created by Jane Golden has now become the nation's largest public art program and a model for other cities throughout the country seeking to help troubled youth.

**Q1(a) — [5 marks] Choose the correct answer:**
i. This passage focuses mostly upon
a) an art program designed to help troubled youth  b) the tourists who come to Philadelphia
c) the many reasons why Philadelphia is a unique city  d) how Jane Golden came up with the idea to start a mural program
ii. In paragraph 1, the author states, "It is said that Philadelphia has more murals than any other city in the world, with the exception of Rome." Using this information, we can conclude that
a) Rome has fewer murals than Philadelphia  b) Philadelphia has fewer murals than Rome
c) Rome has the most beautiful murals in the world  d) Rome and Philadelphia are the only cities with murals in the world
iii. According to the passage, the mural program helps troubled youth by teaching them
a) to be more responsible  b) how to collaborate with others  c) to take pride in their community  d) all of a), b) and c)
iv. As used in paragraph 3, which is the best definition for affluent?
a) popular  b) clean  c) well-known  d) wealthy
v. What types of murals can be found in Philadelphia?
a) portraits of historical heroes  b) cityscapes  c) scenes depicting diverse ethnic groups  d) all of a), b) and c)

**Q1(b) — [5 marks] Answer the following questions:**
