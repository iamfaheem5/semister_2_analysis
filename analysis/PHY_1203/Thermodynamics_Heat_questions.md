## Part A — Actual Previous-Year Questions (Verbatim / Near-Verb from 2020–2023)

> Source: PHY-1203 Year-Final papers 2020–2023 (vision extraction from scanned PDFs). Marks shown as in original. `γ` = ratio of specific heats, `R` = gas constant.

### 2020 — PHY-1203 Year Final

**Q1(a) — [3 marks]**
What do you mean by entropy? Prove that the entropy increases at irreversible process.

**Q1(b) — [7 marks]**
Mention some conditions of adiabatic change. Prove that $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{Constant}$.

**Q1(c) — [4 marks]**
At $0^{\circ}\text{C}$ temperature, a fixed amount of gas is expanded double in volume. If $\gamma=1.4$ then find out the last temperature.

**Q2(a) — [3 marks]**
Describe the first law of thermodynamics. Show that, $dw = P\,dV$.

**Q2(b) — [7 marks]**
Describe each stage of Carnot's cycle and find out its efficiency.

**Q2(c) — [4 marks]**
40 gms of Hydrogen gas at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ are isothermally compressed to one fourth of the original volume. Find the value of work done during the process. Here $R=8.4\,\text{J/gm-mole}$.

### 2021 — PHY-1203 Year Final

**Q1(a) — [3 marks]**
What do you mean by entropy? Prove that entropy is constant in reversible process.

**Q1(b) — [7 marks]**
What is adiabatic change? For adiabatic process prove that $PV^{\gamma} = \text{Constant}$.

**Q1(c) — [4 marks]**
A motor car tyre has a pressure of 2 atmospheres at $25^{\circ}\text{C}$. It is suddenly burst, find the resulting temperature. ($\gamma=1.4$)

**Q2(a) — [3 marks]**
Define: (i) Degrees of freedom (ii) $2^{\text{nd}}$ law of thermodynamics.

**Q2(b) — [7 marks]**
Find the equation of work done for expansion of gas in adiabatic and isothermal process.

**Q2(c) — [4 marks]**
A Carnot engine whose $T_1 = 7^{\circ}\text{C}$ and efficiency of 50%. It is desire to increase of efficiency to 70%. By how many degrees should the temperature of the high be increased?

### 2022 — PHY-1203 Year Final

**Q1(a) — [3 marks]**
State and explain the first and second laws of thermodynamics.

**Q1(b) — [7 marks]**
What are Isothermal and Adiabatic processes? Establish the relation between pressure and volume in the adiabatic process.

**Q1(c) — [4 marks]**
A quantity of air at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ and atmospheric pressure is suddenly compressed to half its original volume. Find the final temperature and pressure. ($\gamma=1.4$)

**Q2(a) — [3 marks]**
Show that the adiabatic curves are $\gamma$ times steeper than isothermal curves.

**Q2(b) — [7 marks]**
What is Carnot cycle? Describe Carnot's cycle each stage and find out its efficiency.

**Q2(c) — [4 marks]**
Calculate the molecular kinetic energy of 1 gm of hydrogen gas at $50^{\circ}\text{C}$, given that the molecular weight of Hydrogen is 2 and $R = 8.3 \times 10^7\,\text{ergs/gm-mol}$.

**Q7(a) — [3 marks] (part of definition trio)**
Define any three: (i) Equipartition of energy (ii) Entropy (iii) Coherent source (iv) damped harmonic oscillation (v) Huygen's principle (vi) double refraction.

**Q7(b) — [7 marks] (Brewster portion overlaps, but thermo part)**
What is Brewster's law? From Brewster's law show that the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular to each other.

**Q7(c) — [4 marks]**
A mass of gas at $80^{\circ}\text{C}$ and 50 atmospheric pressure is adiabatically expanded to 10 times its initial volume. If the ratio of specific heats is 1.4. Calculate the resulting pressure and temperature.

## Part B — 10 Original Practice Questions (Exam-Realistic)

> Same style, marks and format as PHY-1203 finals. Enriches concepts not merely rephrased. Try these **without** looking at the answer vault.

### **[Easy]**

**P1. [3 marks] [Easy]**
Define (i) isothermal process (ii) adiabatic process (iii) isobaric process. Write one necessary condition for each and state whether $dQ=0$ or $dT=0$ holds.

**P2. [3 marks] [Easy]**
State the zeroth, first and second laws of thermodynamics in one sentence each. Give one everyday example for each law.

**P3. [4 marks] [Easy]**
Define entropy $S$ and write its differential form $dS = dQ_{rev}/T$. A block of ice melts at $0^{\circ}\text{C}$ absorbing 336 J/g. Calculate the entropy change per gram. Is this reversible?

### **[Medium]**

**P4. [5 marks] [Medium]**
Derive $C_p - C_v = R$ for one mole of ideal gas using first law and $PV=RT$. Explain where degrees of freedom $f$ enters to give $C_v = \frac{f}{2}R$ and $\gamma = 1+2/f$. Evaluate $\gamma$ for monoatomic and diatomic gases.

**P5. [5 marks] [Medium]**
An ideal gas ($\gamma=1.4$) at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ and 1 atm is reversibly and adiabatically compressed to 1/8 of its original volume. Calculate (i) final pressure (ii) final temperature (iii) work done on the gas if initially 1 mole. Compare the steepness of this adiabat with the isotherm at the starting point.

**P6. [6 marks] [Medium]**
A Carnot engine operates between $400\,\text{K}$ and $300\,\text{K}$ absorbing 600 J per cycle from the hot reservoir. Calculate (i) efficiency (ii) heat rejected (iii) work per cycle. If it is run in reverse as a refrigerator, what is its COP?

### **[Hard]**

**P7. [6 marks] [Hard]**
Prove that entropy remains constant in a reversible adiabatic process but increases in a free expansion (Joule expansion) of an ideal gas. A 2-mole ideal gas doubles its volume by free expansion at 300 K. Calculate $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_2/V_1)$ and explain why $dQ=0$ does not imply $dS=0$ here.

**P8. [7 marks] [Hard]**
Derive the work done $W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\,dV$ for (i) reversible isothermal: $W = nRT\ln(V_2/V_1) = nRT\ln(P_1/P_2)$ and (ii) reversible adiabatic: $W = (P_1V_1 - P_2V_2)/(\gamma-1)$. Show that isothermal work is area under $PV$ curve and discuss why adiabatic work is less than isothermal for same volume expansion from same starting point (use $P-V$ diagram).

**P9. [4 marks] [Hard]**
A car tyre at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ and 3 atm bursts adiabatically. Estimate the final temperature using $T_2 = T_1(P_2/P_1)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$ with $P_2=1\,\text{atm}$, $\gamma=1.4$. What is the temperature drop? Explain why the air feels cold after bursting.

**P10. [5 marks] [Hard]**
Define internal energy $U$, thermodynamic function (state function) and Joule's mechanical equivalent of heat $J$. A 0.5 kg copper block ($c=385\,\text{J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$) falls from 50 m and is stopped; assume its mechanical energy fully converts to thermal energy. Calculate $\Delta T$ and explain how this demonstrates $W = JQ$.
