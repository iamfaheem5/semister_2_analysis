### 2020 Q6(a) — Packing fraction, why <1, which largest [3]

**Packing fraction (APF):** fraction of unit cell volume occupied by hard-sphere atoms: $PF = N_{atoms} \times (4/3)\pi r^3 / V_{cell}$. Less than 1 because spheres cannot fill all space; voids remain (interstitial). Maximum is FCC/HCP $≈0.74$, BCC $0.68$, SC $0.52$, diamond $0.34$. FCC (or HCP, close-packed) largest.

### 2020 Q6(b) + 2022 Q4(b) — Miller indices & $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$ [7]

**Miller indices $(hkl)$:** Reciprocals of intercepts $p,q,r$ on axes in units of $a,b,c$, cleared to smallest integers: $h:k:l =1/p:1/q:1/r$.

**Prove cubic:** Plane $(hkl)$ intercepts $a/h, a/k, a/l$. Equation $h x + k y + l z = a$. Distance from origin (0,0,0) to plane = $| -a|/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$. Adjacent parallel plane through origin reduces to this interplanar spacing. For set at distance $d$, $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$.

### 2020 Q6(c) — Spacing for (111),(112),(113) with lattice constant $a$ [~4]

$d_{111}=a/\sqrt{3}=0.577a$, $d_{112}=a/\sqrt{1+1+4}=a/\sqrt6=0.408a$, $d_{113}=a/\sqrt{11}=0.302a$.

### 2021 Q5(a) — Define PF, calculate for SC [5]

PF definition as above. **SC:** 1 atom/cell (8 corners ×1/8), $2r=a$, $V_{atom}=4/3\pi (a/2)^3= \pi a^3/6$, $V_{cell}=a^3$ → $PF=\pi/6≈0.5236$ (52.36%). Show steps.

### 2021 Q5(b) — Orthorhombic $d =1/\sqrt{h^2/a^2+k^2/b^2+l^2/c^2}$ [6]

Plane intercepts $a/h, b/k, c/l$, equation $h x/a + k y/b + l z/c =1$. Normal distance $d =1/\sqrt{(h/a)^2+(k/b)^2+(l/c)^2}$. Derive via intercept form $x/(a/h)+y/(b/k)+z/(c/l)=1$, distance formula.

### 2021 Q5(c) — Ratio 1:0.71:0.58 for (100),(110),(111) [3]

$d_{100}=a$, $d_{110}=a/\sqrt2=0.707a$, $d_{111}=a/\sqrt3=0.577a$ → normalize to $d_{100}$: $1:0.707:0.577$ ≈ $1:0.71:0.58$.

### 2022 Q5(b) — $a_{bcc}=4r/\sqrt3$, $a_{fcc}=4r/\sqrt2$ [7]

- **BCC:** Body diagonal $=4r$, diagonal $=\sqrt3 a$ → $a=4r/\sqrt3$.
- **FCC:** Face diagonal $=4r$, diagonal $=\sqrt2 a$ → $a=4r/\sqrt2 =2\sqrt2 r$.

Atoms per cell: BCC 2, FCC 4.

### 2022 Q5(c) — Plane cuts $a,2b,3c$ Miller [4]

Intercepts $p=1, q=2, r=3$ in units $a,b,c$. Reciprocals $1, 1/2, 1/3$ → LCM 6 → $6,3,2$. Miller $(632)$. If $2a,3b,1c$ alternative gives $(3,2,6)$.

### 2023 Q5(a) — Band Theory + n/p type [6]

Band formed by splitting of atomic levels into quasi-continuous bands when $N$ atoms interact. **Valence band** filled, **conduction band** empty, gap $E_g$. Conductor $E_g≈0$ overlap, semiconductor small $~1eV$, insulator large $>3eV$.

- **n-type:** Doped donor (P in Si), extra electrons, Fermi level near conduction, majority electrons.
- **p-type:** Acceptor (B in Si), holes, Fermi near valence, majority holes.
Diagram: bands with Fermi levels.

### 2023 Q5(b)/ Q6(b) — X-ray interplanar spacing & Bragg's law [5/7]

X-ray wavelength $∼d$, diffraction from planes satisfies **Bragg's law:** $2d\sin\theta = n\lambda$ ( $n$ integer, $\theta$ glancing angle). Derive: path difference between reflections from successive planes $=2d\sin\theta$, constructive when $=n\lambda$.

Given $\lambda$ and $\theta$, $d=n\lambda/(2\sin\theta)$.

**Diffraction of X-rays:** CTR: crystal acts as grating; Laue or Bragg method.

### 2023 Q5(c) — Sodium BCC lattice constant [3]

Density $\rho=9.6×10^2$ kg/m³ (given, note typical 970; use given), $M=23$ g/mol $=0.023$ kg/mol, $n=2$ atoms/cell (BCC). $\rho = n M/(N_A a^3)$ → $a^3 = n M/(N_A \rho) =2×0.023/(6.022×10^{23}×960)=7.96×10^{-29}$ → $a=4.30×10^{-10}$m $=4.3$ Å. If $\rho=0.96×10^3$? Some paper uses $9.6×10^2$ =960, result ~4.3Å, literature 4.225Å. Method same.

### Also 2023 Q6(a) — Features of Miller indices [4]

Features: (i) parallel planes same → family {hkl}, (ii) perpendicular to vector $h\mathbf{a}^*+k\mathbf{b}^*+l\mathbf{c}^*$, (iii) small integers, (iv) intercept at $\infty$→0, (v) negative bar, (vi) determines $d$ and diffraction.
